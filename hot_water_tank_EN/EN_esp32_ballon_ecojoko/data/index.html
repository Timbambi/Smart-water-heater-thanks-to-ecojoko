Learned hot water need

One point per cycle (from end of off-peak hours to end of off-peak hours), over the last 35 days kept by the ESP32. Tank plan (full mode)

The tank is seen as a battery: when the thermostat cuts, it is full. The energy injected between two "full" events gives the real consumption (the daily need), learned without a probe. The weather (Open-Meteo) provides the forecast sun and temperature.
At night: the plan only heats what tomorrow's sun will not cover. During the day: a check before the evening verifies that the reserve plus enough to last the evening remains; otherwise it heats just the shortfall, at the last moment.
Observation: computes and displays without controlling anything. Active: drives the off-peak hours. At the slightest doubt (weather missing, insufficient learning, restart): classic Full behaviour.
Deficit: estimated missing energy for the tank to be full. 24 h need: expected consumption. To heat: energy the plan adds tonight. Expected solar: energy the forecast sun can bring. Avoidable HC: off-peak hours the plan would have avoided (observation).

Heating log

Each block is a continuous heating run of the tank (thermostat pauses shorter than 15 min do not split it), with the reason it happened: HC (off-peak hours), Solar (the surplus makes heating profitable), Catch-up (the plan heats in peak hours to keep the reserve before the evening: heating that would not have happened without it). The timeline covers one cycle, from the end of the HC to the end of the next HC; the grey band is the off-peak window. A gap in the grey band = avoided HC. The green line under the blocks shows when the relay is on (state confirmed by the Shelly, not the command sent): a relay on with no block above it means the thermostat was not heating (tank full) or the heating was too short to be recorded. If the Shelly stops answering, the line is neither extended nor cut right away. Net vs all-HC compares what was paid with the cost of the same energy heated in HC: negative = extra cost. The log is kept in memory (about 14 days) and copied to flash; it only covers Full mode, and the heating in progress during a restart is lost, unless you use Save now just before (System tab, "Backup and restore"): the button also writes the heating in progress to flash. It is read back as a finished block, and the heating that resumes forms a new block. If you reinstall the flash files (LittleFS), everything is erased: export the heating log first, then import it afterwards.

Shelly temperature

This is the temperature inside the Shelly's housing (its chip), not the water. Orange curve = maximum temperature over each 5 min slot; blue area = average tank power. Healthy installation: the temperature rises at the start of heating, then settles on a plateau, and drops when the relay opens. Bad connection or load too close to the limit: a stronger rise that keeps climbing, or a plateau that differs from one night to another for the same power. The last 48 hours are kept in memory: they restart from zero when the ESP32 restarts.

Backup and restore

Before an update: "Save now" writes to flash the heating log (with the heating in progress) and the day's counters, including the forecast's need and solar measurement (read back at boot then deleted). That is enough if you only flash the program. If you also reinstall the flash files (LittleFS), everything is erased: first export the JSON files below, then, after flashing, re-enter Wi-Fi, Ecojoko and the Shelly IP and import them.
Settings (JSON): mode, off-peak hours, tariffs, powers, quota, timer, alarm delay, quiet window and remote silence duration, Shelly thermal protection and forecast (tank volume and capacity, reserve, check time, position, peak power). Never exported: Wi-Fi and password, Ecojoko email and password, Shelly IP, static IP, clock, watchdog pins.
Import: a single button, several files at once possible (Ctrl/Shift, or multiple selection on a phone), the type of each file is recognised automatically (days, months, habits, heating log, settings). The import merges: a day, month or block already present is never overwritten. The current month is only restored if it is still empty. The current day is restored only if it is more complete than the current counters (each counter keeps the larger value, without double counting). Imported settings replace the current values and apply immediately, mode included. CSV files cannot be imported (they are for Excel). Maximum size: 60 KB.

Operating mode

Automatic: aims for a quota of heating hours per cycle ("Quota (h)" setting in Configuration). While the quota is not reached: heats in peak hours only if solar makes it profitable, and off-peak hours are forced at the last moment to reach it. Quota reached: the tank only heats on a real free solar surplus (bonus).
Full: heats during all off-peak hours (the tank's thermostat cuts when it is full) + opportunistic solar the rest of the time, without a quota. This is the mode the Forecast acts on.
Manual: you control the Shelly yourself (buttons or daily timer).
Consumption without Shelly: Ecojoko monitoring only, no relay is controlled.

The mode without Shelly does not contact or control any relay.

Advanced settings

Watchdog (ESP32-C3)

The problem stays displayed on the page and in the log; only the ring is silenced. If the main program freezes during the window, the C3 stays quiet until the end of the window and then sounds if the problem lasts. Maximum window of 12 h.

Shelly thermal protection

The Shelly measures its own internal temperature (curve in the System tab). If it reaches the cut-off threshold, or if the Shelly itself reports overheating, the ESP32 cuts the relay and sends no more switch-on commands, whatever the mode (except Monitoring, where the relay is not controlled). It only resumes once the minimum pause has elapsed (it restarts from zero while it is still too hot) and the Shelly is below the resume temperature. If the Shelly stops answering, the pause is maintained. The Shelly's own alarm always cuts, even with a threshold of 0. Set the threshold above the curve's normal plateau, but below the Shelly's limit (see its datasheet): the dotted line on the chart shows the margin.
Audible alarm: above this threshold (to be set higher than the cut-off), the watchdog buzzer sounds immediately and an alert is written to the error log. It also sounds if the Shelly stops responding during a thermal protection (it was too hot, then no more news). A message is written to the error log at each cut-off.

Network

Choose an IP outside the router's DHCP range. If the gateway does not answer at boot, the ESP32 automatically goes back to DHCP. A change restarts the ESP32.
